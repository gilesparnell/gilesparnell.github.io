Lepus Proprietary Trading

An Australian firm offering trader training and a “funding challenge”. A friend asked me to look at it, so I read the site properly and checked it against the public registers. Here's what's actually on offer, and the six questions worth asking before any money moves.

ⓘ

My own notes, based on publicly available records as at August 2026. General information, not financial advice, and not a recommendation to trade or not to trade. Verify everything independently before committing money.

The short version

It isn't a scam. But it also isn't what “prop firm” normally means, and the marketing leans on that gap.

Read it as a paid trading course that may lead to a funded account — not as a funding opportunity. Judge it on whether the education is worth the price, because on profit split, transparency and company age it loses to the offshore competition, and it asks for $2,000 of your real money that they don't.

What's actually on offer

Two products bolted together:

01
Paid training and mentoring
A 16+ module course with the head trader, one-to-one sessions, a Discord community and monthly boardroom sessions. The price is not published anywhere on the site — you book a call to find out.
02
The “Funding Challenge” — no participation fee
You open a live retail account with OX Securities, deposit a minimum of A$2,000 of your own money, and trade it for three months at 1:30 leverage. Pass, and you're offered a funded account at a 50% profit split.
The bit that matters

This is not the FTMO-style model, where you pay a fee and risk only that fee on a simulated account. Your own capital is genuinely at risk in a live market account. “No participation fee”, “no maximum drawdown” and “no minimum targets” all sound generous — but you don't need a drawdown rule when the money being drawn down belongs to the trader.

Where it's genuinely strong

  • + StrengthA real regulatory footprint Wolverton Investment Group Pty Ltd, ACN 639 257 613, Corporate Authorised Representative 1314535, a Melbourne street address and a named principal with a public profile. Most offshore prop shops have none of that.
  • + StrengthThe rules are sane, not predatory No trailing drawdown, no consistency rule, no 30-day profit target, no clause engineered to void a payout. Grid and martingale banned, five open trades maximum, FXBlue monitoring. That reads as a firm assessing skill, not one designing failure.
  • + StrengthYour $2,000 stays yours If you aren't selected, you withdraw whatever's left. No non-refundable fee burned — unlike serial challenge-retakers spending $500 a go.
  • + StrengthThree months of live trading is a better filter More meaningful than a ten-day simulated sprint, and arguably better for the trader's development too.
  • + StrengthThe education looks like the real product And by most accounts it's competently delivered, in Australian hours, by an actual named person.

Where it should give you pause

  • ! Concern50% split is half the market rate FTMO, FundedNext and similar pay 80–95%. You're paying a 30–45 point premium for the training and the Australian wrapper.
  • ! ConcernThe company is far newer than it reads ABN Lookup shows the ABN active only from 30 January 2025, and both business names — “Lepus Prop” and “Wolverton Investment Group” — registered 18 September 2025. The “operating since 2013 / 10+ year track record” line that circulates on third-party sites doesn't appear on the register, and I couldn't find it on the firm's own site either. Whatever the history behind it, the current trading entity is under a year old.
  • ! ConcernThe AFSL isn't theirs AFSL 460940 belongs to AFSL Holdings Australia Pty Ltd, a dealer group that authorises third parties. Lepus is one Corporate Authorised Representative among many. That's legal and common — but “AFSL 460940” on the site reads as though Lepus is licensed. It isn't, and the authorisation covers financial product advice and arranging to deal, which is not the same as being licensed to run a funded-trader programme.
  • ! ConcernAn obvious conflict goes unaddressed A firm that routes every applicant into one named broker for a minimum of three months has a plausible revenue line in introducing-broker rebates on trading volume. I have no evidence either way — what I can verify is that no commission or rebate disclosure appears anywhere on the site, and that's a question with a one-sentence answer. If such an arrangement does exist, the firm earns whether or not anyone passes, which sits badly against “the firm is only profitable once the trader is.”
  • ! ConcernNo pricing published, anywhere Not for the course, the coaching, or the in-office sessions. “We do not intend to profit from educating you and thus charge an extremely fair price” is a sentence doing a lot of work with no number attached. Pricing that requires a booked call is a sales-funnel pattern.
  • ! ConcernThe funded-account terms are invisible What capital, whose money, what platform, what payout mechanics, what contract — none of it is on the site. That's $2,000 and three months committed to an outcome whose terms you haven't seen.
  • ! ConcernOX Securities is a mixed bag ASIC-regulated in Australia, but it also runs a St Vincent & the Grenadines entity, has recurring reports of withdrawal delays and a US$40 wire fee, and was added to the CFTC RED List in May 2025 for servicing US clients while unregistered. Which entity holds the $2,000 matters a great deal.
  • ! ConcernThe numbers contradict each other The traders page advertises “no experience necessary” alongside earning “up to 80% of the performance”. The challenge page says 50%. Those two figures should not disagree.

Six questions to ask first

Get every one of these in writing, not on a call, before paying anything or depositing anything.

  1. Do you receive any commission, rebate or introducing-broker payment from OX Securities based on my trading volume? How much per lot? Decisive
  2. Which OX Securities entity holds my account — the ASIC-licensed Australian one, or the St Vincent one?
  3. What is the total cost of the training, and is completing it a prerequisite for entering the Funding Challenge?
  4. Send me the funded-trader agreement now, before I deposit. What capital, what platform, what payout schedule, what termination rights?
  5. How many people have started the Funding Challenge, how many were funded, and how many have actually been paid out? Decisive
  6. What are the real selection criteria after three months, given there are no minimum targets?
How to read the answers

Questions 1 and 5 decide it. A firm confident in its own model answers both without friction. Evasion on either — particularly the payout numbers — is your answer.

The bottom line

If you want structured education, in your own timezone, from a real named person, and you'd pay for that on its own merits, this is a reasonable place to spend money — provided the price turns out to be reasonable once they tell you what it is.

If what you actually want is funded trading capital, it's the wrong door. You'd be handing over $2,000 of live risk and three months for half the split the offshore firms pay, from an entity registered under a year ago, on terms you won't see until after you've committed.

Nothing here says don't do it. It says don't do it blind.
